Etsy shows you views and favourites on the dashboard, so that's what you'll chase. Neither one is what the algorithm ranks you on, and neither one pays. Here are the numbers that actually decide it — with the thresholds, the kill lines, and the schedule.

① The metric that lies

Favourites are “nice”.
Orders are “I need this”.

Etsy ranks listings by predicted likelihood of conversion. Listing Quality Score is 30–40% of ranking and is built from clicks, favourites, and purchases from search. A listing with heavy favourites and no orders isn't "warming up" — it's actively telling Etsy the listing is a bad bet.

Conversion = Orders ÷ Visits

The diagnostic that tells you what's broken. Compare favourites to orders. Many favourites, few orders means the thumbnail and title are doing their job and the offer is failing — price, shipping cost, or the photos on the listing page. Few favourites and few views is the opposite problem: nobody is finding you, so it's SEO, not offer. Same low conversion, two completely different fixes.
Expect different rates by source. Etsy search 2–4% · Etsy ads 1–3% · external traffic 0.5–2% · repeat customers 5–15%. That last figure is why the email list and the Deadpan Society matter more than any ad.

The trap specific to marketplaces. Etsy needs 30–90 days to rank a listing. Anything tied to a trend with a shorter life than that cannot possibly work here — the listing ranks the week the trend dies. Etsy is an evergreen channel. Trends belong on TikTok.
④ The counterintuitive one

Do not touch
a new listing
for 30 days.

The instinct — tweak the title, swap the photo, renew for a recency bump — is the single most common way beginners kneecap themselves. Etsy studies how real buyers interact with a listing before deciding where to rank it. Every edit restarts that learning.

The rule. Publish complete, then leave it alone for 30 days. Edits index in 24–48 hours but meaningful ranking change takes 30–90 days, so anything you change inside the first month is a change you can never measure. Then edit one variable at a time — title, or thumbnail, or price. Never two.
Renewing is not a ranking hack. It costs $0.20 and buys nothing but a tiny freshness signal. Renewing a listing that isn't converting just pays to keep a losing bet on the table.

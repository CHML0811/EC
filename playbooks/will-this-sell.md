# Will this actually sell? — an honest accounting

You asked twice. Straight answer: **no, I'm not sure. Nobody can be.** What follows is the
separation I should have written earlier — what I actually verified, what I assumed and
presented as if verified, and the one number I never got.

---

| Claim | Evidence |
|---|---|
| The category has real demand | ~500 listings under "funny award certificates", with **demonstrated high sales volume** on the top items over six months ([market](https://www.etsy.com/uk/market/funny_award_certificates)) |

That's a genuinely encouraging picture: **people demonstrably buy this kind of thing, in this
format, on this platform.**

## The number I never got

**I checked competition but never search volume.** I don't know whether "office awards" gets
500 searches a month or 50,000. That data sits behind eRank and EverBee and isn't published
anywhere I could reach.

That's a real gap in the work, and it's the single thing most worth closing before you spend
anything. **You can close it in ten minutes, free** — see below.

## Risks I under-weighted

- **Zero reviews.** New shops convert worse than established ones, full stop. The first sale
  is the hardest one you will ever make.
- **Digital buyers comparison-shop.** A $24 item sits in a grid next to $9 ones. The gap is
  visible in a way it wouldn't be for a physical product.
- **Seasonality cuts both ways.** Office awards peak around year-end. That's favourable
  timing now, but it also means a quiet January tells you nothing.

## Do this before you spend the $20

**1. Get the number I couldn't (10 min, free).**
Sign up for [eRank](https://erank.com)'s free tier — 100 keyword lookups a day. Check:
`office awards` · `funny awards` · `employee awards` · `printable awards`

- **Under ~1,000 searches/month** → the niche is too thin. Tell me and we change direction.
- **1,000–20,000** → normal and workable. Proceed.
- **Over 20,000** → good, but expect the competition to be tougher than 500 listings suggests.

**2. Read the competitors' review counts (5 min, free).**
Open the top 3 listings for "funny office awards". Reviews ≈ **5–10% of sales**. A listing
with 200 reviews has sold roughly 2,000–4,000 units. That tells you the ceiling better than
any keyword tool.

**3. Then decide.** If both look thin, don't spend the $20 — we pick a different niche with
the same machinery, which now takes days rather than weeks.

## One recommendation I'm changing

**Launch at $18, not $24.** I want to be explicit that this reverses what I wrote earlier.

The reasoning: Etsy gives a new listing a boost for 14–21 days, and ranking is driven by
**conversion rate and sales velocity inside that window**. Early sales are worth more than
early margin, because ranking compounds for months afterwards and $6 doesn't.

- At $24: you keep $21.27, and might make 2–3 sales in the boost window.
- At $18: you keep $15.72, and are far likelier to make 8–10.

Ten sales at $18 beats three at $24 — not for the $57 difference, but because ten sales and
ten reviews put the listing somewhere three sales never will. **Raise to $24 once you have
about ten reviews.** That's the moment the premium becomes defensible to a buyer.

$18 is still above the $8–15 template packs, which is right — the kit genuinely contains more.

## The honest expected outcome

**Most new Etsy listings sell nothing in the first month.** That is the normal case, not the
failure case. Ranking takes 30–90 days and the boost window is only the first three weeks.

Realistic for 60 days, if the volume check comes back healthy: **0–10 sales.** Possibly zero.
The 30-day plan already says this and it's worth repeating here.

**What the $20 buys is not a business. It's the answer to the question you keep asking** —
and it's the cheapest way to get it. The alternative isn't avoiding risk; it's never finding
out, after all the work is already done and paid for.

If it sells: you have a validated product with 89% margins and a repeatable machine.
If it doesn't: you're out $20 and you know within 60 days, with every asset reusable for the
next attempt.

**That asymmetry is the only real argument for pressing publish. Not my confidence — I don't
have enough of it to lend you.**

# Etsy or our own website?

You asked why we're paying Etsy's setup and fees instead of just selling on our own site.
Good question, and checking it properly turned up two things — one you were right about, and
one I had wrong.

---

## First: the correction

**I have been quoting Etsy's cut as ~23%. That is wrong for most sales.** The real
structure ([Etsy fees 2026](https://blog.marmalead.com/etsy-fees-explained/),
[breakdown](https://craftybase.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-etsy-fees)):

| Fee | Amount | On a $24 sale |
|---|---|---:|
| Listing | $0.20 per sale (and every 4 months unsold) | $0.20 |
| Transaction | 6.5% | $1.56 |
| Payment processing (US) | 3% + $0.25 | $0.97 |
| **Subtotal — every sale** | **≈11.4%** | **$2.73** |
| Offsite Ads | 15% — **only when the sale came from an Etsy ad click** | $3.60 |

So on the kit: **you keep $21.27 on an organic sale, not $18.50.** My figure was roughly the
offsite-ads case presented as if it were normal. It isn't — Offsite Ads is **optional and
opt-out-able until your shop passes $10k in 12 months**, after which it's mandatory at 12%.

And you were right about setup: **new sellers pay a one-time $15–29 fee**, which I never
mentioned. Currently the first 40 listings are free, which offsets about $8 of it.
([setup fees](https://www.thecraftmap.com/blog/etsy-shop-setup-fees))

**Total to get the kit live on Etsy: about $15–29, once.** Then $2.73 per sale.

---

## Now the real question

We already *have* the website. `site/index.html` is built, the Shopify store exists, the
uploader will fill it in one command. So this isn't "build one or not" — it's **which one
gets the first sale.**

Per-sale, your own site does win:

| Route | Fixed cost | Fee on a $24 sale | You keep |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| **Etsy** (organic) | $15–29 once | $2.73 | **$21.27** |
| **Shopify Basic** + Stripe | **$39/mo — $468/yr** | ~$1.00 | **$23.00** |
| **Gumroad** | $0/mo | ~$2.40 | $21.60 |
| **Payhip / Lemon Squeezy** | $0/mo | ~$1.70–2.20 | ~$21.80–22.30 |

Own site keeps **$1.73 more per sale** than Etsy. So why not?

### Because the website is not the hard part. The buyers are.

Etsy has ~90 million active buyers already typing "funny office awards" into a search box.
Your own site has **zero visitors** on day one and forever after, until you pay for them or
earn them.

Do the arithmetic on that $1.73 advantage:

- **Shopify Basic costs $468/year.** At $1.73 extra per sale, you need **271 sales a year —
  about 23 a month — just to break even on the platform fee.** Before a single visitor.
- **You are currently on Advanced at $399/month**, which is $4,788/year. Break-even against
  Etsy there is **2,768 sales a year.**

And that assumes traffic is free. It isn't. Selling a $24 digital product to cold traffic on
Meta or Google realistically costs **$10–30 per customer** for an unknown brand. Even at an
optimistic $10:

> **Own site:** $23.00 − $10.00 traffic = **$13.00**
> **Etsy:** **$21.27**, traffic included

### The reframe that settles it

**Etsy's $2.73 is not a platform fee. It's customer acquisition, at $2.73 a customer.**

There is no ad platform on earth that will sell you a buyer for $2.73. That's the entire
trade, and at your stage it isn't close.

---

## Where your own site genuinely wins

All of these are real — they're just *later*:

| | Why the site wins there |
|---|---|
| **Repeat buyers** | They already know you. No acquisition cost, so the 11% buys nothing. |
| **Bulk / departmental orders** | On a $575 order, Etsy's cut is ~$65. Worth avoiding — which is exactly why Form 7B lives on the Shopify site, not Etsy. |
| **Traffic you own** | Pinterest, SEO, the email list. Once it exists, sending it to Etsy pays 11% for nothing. |
| **Brand credibility** | A real site makes a bulk buyer take you seriously. Etsy alone doesn't. |

So the plan isn't Etsy *instead of* a website. It's **Etsy acquires, the site retains and
takes the large orders** — which is what's already built.

---

## What to actually do

1. **List on Etsy.** ~$20 once, then $2.73 a sale, and it comes with buyers.
2. **Downgrade Shopify to Basic today.** You're paying **$399/month** for features nothing in
   this project uses. That's **$360 a month wasted — about 17 kit sales' worth of profit,
   every month.** You are worrying about a $20 Etsy fee while burning $4,788 a year.
3. **Consider dropping Shopify entirely for now.** For a purely digital product, Gumroad or
   Payhip cost **$0/month** and take a similar cut to Etsy. Shopify only earns its keep once
   the physical line is live or bulk orders start.
4. **Put every Etsy buyer on an email list.** That's how you stop renting the audience. The
   second sale to the same person is the one where owning your own site finally pays.

**The honest summary:** your instinct is right for month six and wrong for month one. Etsy's
fee is the cheapest customer acquisition available to someone with no audience — and the
$399/month Shopify plan is a far bigger leak than anything Etsy charges.

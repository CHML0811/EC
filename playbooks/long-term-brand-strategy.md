# Long-Term Brand & Goods Strategy — Deadpan Goods

**Market: United States. Currency: USD.**

The short-term plan makes money. This one decides whether you still have a business in
three years — because the default outcome for a gag-gift store is death by 18 months, and
it's worth being precise about why.

---

## 1 · The structural problem (name it before solving it)

Gag gifts have **near-zero repeat purchase**. Someone buys the shark suit once, ever.
That means **LTV ≈ AOV**, and a business where LTV equals AOV is not a brand — it's a
permanent rental agreement with the TikTok algorithm. CAC only ever rises. When the algorithm
shifts, revenue goes to zero in a week.

That is exactly how these stores die. Not from bad products — from **no second purchase.**

**The escape is a reframe:**

> You are not a store that sells gag gifts.
> You are **the place people go when they need to be funny.**

Nobody needs a shark suit twice. But *everybody* faces **4–6 gifting occasions a year** —
birthdays, Secret Santa, Father's Day, bachelor parties, housewarmings, white elephant — and
every one of them triggers the same panic: *"I need something funny and I have no idea what."*

Own that moment and the repeat purchase problem solves itself.

---

## 2 · Three horizons

| Horizon | When | Goal | Success looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1 · Prove** | 0–6 mo | Win one season, validate the loop | 500 orders, one product past all gates, CAC < margin |
| **H2 · Compound** | 6–18 mo | Build owned audience + repeat occasions | 25k email/SMS list, **30% repeat rate**, 3 seasons hit |
| **H3 · Own** | 18–36 mo | Own the category, build real equity | Proprietary products, brand search volume, sellable asset |

**Do not skip H1.** Most founders try to build H3 branding before proving anyone will buy.
Trend-chasing is completely fine in H1 — it's how you find out what works. It's only fatal if
you're *still* doing only that in H2.

---

## 3 · The goods ladder

Every product has one job. Stop expecting one product to do all four.

| Tier | Job | Margin | Examples | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Traffic** | Buy attention | Low (30–40%) | Shark suit, window silhouettes | Must demo in 3s. Break-even is acceptable — it's marketing spend. |
| **Margin** | Make the money | High (60–70%) | Uncle Pack, Haunt The Whole House | Every ad points here. Bundles are where you actually profit. |
| **Retention** | Create horizon 2 | Recurring | The Deadpan Society (below) | The only tier that compounds. |
| **Moat** | Defensibility | Highest | Original designs you own | Cannot be copied off AliExpress. |

**Portfolio target by end of H2:** 30% traffic · 45% margin · 15% retention · 10% moat.

Today you are **100% traffic + margin.** That's correct for H1 and dangerous past month six.

---

## 4 · The retention product (the single most important build)

**"The Deadpan Society" — $79/year.**

> You will need a funny gift roughly five times this year. You will forget every single time.
> We won't.
>
> Members get a gift-ready item delivered before each major occasion, first access to drops,
> free shipping all year, and the quiet confidence of never again buying a gas-station candle
> in a panic.

Why this is the whole game:
- Converts one-time buyers into **recurring revenue** — the thing that makes a business an asset
- Turns your worst weakness (no repeat purchase) into your strongest moat
- Makes CAC math work: you can pay 3× more per customer than any competitor and still win
- Gives you **demand you can forecast**, which fixes supplier negotiation and cash flow

**Build it once you have ~1,000 buyers.** Not before — you need to know which occasions
actually convert.

---

## 5 · The moat — four things worth owning

Dropshipped novelty has **zero defensibility**. Anyone can list the same AliExpress SKU
tomorrow at $4 less. So build assets that can't be cloned:

1. **Original IP.** Stop reselling other people's products. Create a recurring character or
   design line *you own* — a mascot, a running joke, a signature format. Pop Mart built a
   billion-dollar business on one owned character (Labubu). You can't sell theirs; you can own
   yours. This is the highest-value asset you can build.
2. **The list.** Email and SMS are the only audience you're not renting. **Target 25k by month
   18.** A platform ban should be survivable, not fatal.
3. **The occasion.** Win the search "funny gift for [X]" and you own the moment of intent.
4. **The voice.** Deadpan is genuinely hard to sustain. Most competitors go loud and cheap
   within a month. Consistency here compounds into something people recognize before they read
   the logo.

## 6 · Goods roadmap

| Phase | Add | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Now – Q4** | Halloween + Christmas/Secret Santa seasonals | Prove the loop on the two biggest gag-gift moments |
| **Q1** | Occasion **gift sets** by recipient (uncle, coworker, new homeowner) | Recipient-based beats product-based for gifters |
| **Q2** | **First original design line** — your own art, POD, high margin | Start the moat |
| **Q3** | **Deadpan Society** membership | Recurring revenue |
| **Q4 (yr 2)** | Proprietary manufactured product (MOQ order) | Real margin, real defensibility |
| **Yr 3** | Retail/wholesale, licensing your own IP | Brand equity, exit optionality |

**Category expansion rule:** only add a category if the *same buyer* buys it for the *same
reason*. Gag gifts → funny home decor → funny drinkware ✅. Gag gifts → phone cases ❌.

## 7 · Brand building, concretely

- **The voice never breaks.** Deadpan in listings, emails, comments, support replies, refunds.
  A brand is just consistency somebody noticed.
- **Name your people.** Members, not customers. A tribe forwards things; a customer list doesn't.
- **Publish, don't just post.** One recurring format people return for — "The Worst Gift I Ever
  Received," reader submissions, an annual awards post. Content people seek out beats content
  the algorithm has to push.
- **UGC is the proof.** Nobody believes your photo of a shark suit. They believe a stranger's
  cookout video. Systematically ask for it post-purchase.
- **Never discount.** Ever. Discounting a $26 gag gift teaches people to wait and destroys the
  premium the joke depends on. Use bundles, limited drops, and member access instead.
- **Refunds are marketing.** "30 days, no interrogation." A generous refund on a $26 item costs
  less than one bad comment thread under a viral video.

## 8 · What actually kills this business

| Risk | Warning sign | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Platform dependency** | >70% traffic from one channel | Build the list. Target <50% from any single source by month 12. |
| **Commoditization** | Competitors at your price in 60 days | Move up the ladder — bundles, then owned designs |
| **IP takedown** | Any character, brand, or likeness | Hard veto in the scorecard. Never negotiate this one. |
| **Seasonal cash crunch** | Jan–Mar is brutal in gifting | Bank Q4 cash. Budget for a lean Q1. |
| **Supplier collapse** | Single supplier on a hero product | Two suppliers per hero, always |
| **Founder burnout** | The weekly content treadmill | Systematize by month 6 — templates, batching, UGC, then hire |

## 9 · Milestones worth aiming at

| By | Target |
|---|---|
| Month 3 | 500 orders · 1 product past all gates · CAC < margin |
| Month 6 | $10k/mo · 5k list · 2 seasons hit · supplier redundancy |
| Month 12 | $30k/mo · 15k list · **20% repeat rate** · own design line launched |
| Month 18 | $50k/mo · 25k list · Deadpan Society live · <50% traffic from any one channel |
| Month 36 | Proprietary product · brand search volume · **sellable at 3–5× SDE** |

**The one number that decides everything: repeat purchase rate.** Below 15%, you own a
storefront that rents attention. Above 30%, you own a brand — and a brand is the only version
of this that's worth anything when you want to stop running it.
